## 1. “Is €70k enough to live in Amsterdam?”

### A — No link

Depends what “enough” means. For a single person renting alone in Amsterdam, **€70k gross** can work if you’re careful, but housing will eat a huge chunk. Shared housing or living just outside the ring changes the picture a lot. Couples with two incomes are usually more comfortable than singles at the same number. Rough check: estimate net → subtract realistic rent + insurance + OV → see what’s left for food and buffer. If you’re coming with family or high debt payments, run the numbers before signing anything.

## 2. “Amsterdam or Rotterdam?”

### A — No link

Different trade-offs. **Amsterdam:** denser jobs/international scene, harder/more expensive housing. **Rotterdam:** often more space for money, different vibe, still solid for many internationals—especially if your employer is there or you’re OK with travel. Decide on: commute to *your* job, rent budget, kids/schools, and whether you need the AMS social scene. Visit both if you can; Reddit opinions skew hard either way.

## 3. “What do I need to do after arriving?”

### A — No link

Typical sequence (order can vary by municipality): temporary address → register with the municipality (BRP) → get **BSN** → arrange **health insurance** within the required window → DigiD when eligible → bank account → employer/payroll details. Don’t wait on insurance. Keep passport + housing proof handy for appointments. Check your gemeente’s site for exact booking steps—they differ.

## 4. “How does Dutch health insurance work?”

### A — No link

Once you’re a resident (timing matters), basic **zorgverzekering** is generally mandatory. You pick a basic package from an insurer; deductibles (eigen risico) apply. Employer usually doesn’t “cover you forever” like some US plans—you typically arrange your own policy. Kids have different rules. For exact start dates and exceptions, use official government/insurance info—not forum guesses.

## 5. “Is the 30% ruling still worth it?”

### A — No link

Often yes for eligible people because it can meaningfully change net pay—but **eligibility, duration, and rules change**, and not everyone qualifies. “Worth it” also depends on salary, family situation, and how long you’ll stay. Don’t assume you’ll get it because a friend did. Check Belastingdienst guidance and, for your case, a tax advisor—especially if you’re renegotiating an offer.

## 6. “Moving from South Africa — what should I prepare?”

### A — No link

Think in two buckets: **NL arrival admin** (visa/permit path, housing, registration, insurance, bank) and **ZA wrap-up** (banking, tax residency questions, documents, apostilles/translations if needed). Non-EU paths need more lead time than EU movers. Keep certified copies of key docs. Scams target newcomers on housing—never wire deposits to unknown private landlords without verification.

## 7. “Best bank for an expat?”

### A — No link

There’s no universal “best.” Compare: account opening with your ID/BSN situation, English app quality, fees, and whether you need business banking later. Many people start with a big retail bank for payroll; some add a fintech later. Opening before BSN can be limited—ask what each bank requires *now*.

## 8. “How hard is it to make friends in NL?”

### A — No link

It can feel slower than in some cultures—people often have established friend groups. Clubs, sports, language cafés, coworker invites, and repeated small hangouts work better than one big “find friends” night. Dutch directness isn’t hostility. Expect months, not weeks. Partner’s network or parent groups change the curve.

## 9. “What salary do I need for HSM?”

### A — No link

Highly Skilled Migrant salary thresholds are **set amounts that change** and depend on age/category (and sometimes reduced thresholds). Don’t use an outdated Reddit number. Check the **current IND** tables for your situation, and remember employer sponsorship / recognized sponsor rules matter as much as the euro figure.

## 10. “How expensive is childcare?”

### A — No link

Can be a major budget line—especially daycare. Costs vary by hours, provider, and whether you get **kinderopvangtoeslag** (depends on situation). International schools are a different price world from local daycare. Get quotes early; waiting lists are real in some cities.
